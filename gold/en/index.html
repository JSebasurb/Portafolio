Design tips

Minimum wall

Use walls of at least 1.2 mm so the part is strong.

Overhangs

Angles beyond 45° over empty space usually need supports.

Tolerances

Leave 0.2 to 0.3 mm of clearance between parts that must fit or move.

Orientation

Layers are weaker between each other: orient the part according to the load it will carry.

Holes

Holes come out slightly smaller; for screws, add about 0.2 mm to the diameter.

Maximum size

Up to 230 × 150 × 140 mm. Larger parts can be split and glued.

2026-10-16 · ETL · Data

Medallion architecture in 5 minutes

Bronze, Silver and Gold: a simple way to organize a data pipeline.

Medallion architecture organizes data into three layers according to quality. It keeps a pipeline orderly, traceable and easy to reprocess.

Bronze stores data exactly as it arrives from the source, unmodified. If something fails later, you can always come back here and start over.

Silver holds clean data: fixed types, duplicates removed and validation rules applied. It is the reliable base for any analysis.

Gold stores business-ready tables: aggregates, indicators and views designed for dashboards and reports.

I applied it in the SIVIGILA ETL and in this very portfolio, where bronze keeps the originals, silver the clean source code and gold the published site.

From a web form to a spreadsheet with n8n

A basic workflow to save every contact automatically.

A very useful first workflow in n8n is receiving data from a web form and saving it to Google Sheets with no manual work.

Step 1: create a Webhook node using the POST method. n8n gives you a test URL and a production URL; the production one only works while the workflow is active.

2026-11-05 · ML · Signals

Why the envelope improved my fault classifier

With the raw signal the model scored 61%; with the envelope, 74%. What I learned with simulated data.

I trained a model to detect bearing faults from vibration. With features from the raw signal, 5-fold cross-validation scored 61%: better than guessing (25% with four classes), but far from useful.

The problem was the signal. A raceway fault shows up as repeated impulses at a known frequency, but in the raw signal those impulses are mixed with the normal vibration of the shaft. Taking the envelope, that is, the absolute value of the signal without its mean, concentrates the impulse energy at the fault frequency, where it can be measured.

That single decision raised cross-validation to 74%. Severe faults are almost always detected; mild ones are still hard. What matters is that the result was measured on data kept apart from training, and that the page runs the same calculation as Python.

The lesson: before changing models, check what information you are giving them. The bottleneck is often how the data is described.

2026-11-05 · Production · Indicators

OEE: the indicator people most often misread

Availability × performance × quality, and the most common mistake when defining the ideal cycle time.

OEE multiplies three factors: availability (the time the machine actually runs), performance (how close it runs to its ideal cycle time) and quality (the share of good parts). An OEE of 100% would mean producing with no stops, at ideal speed and with no defects.

The most common mistake is defining the ideal cycle time badly. If you take an overly optimistic theoretical value, performance looks low even when the machine runs well. In the dashboard I built I used the lowest cycle observed in the data; that is a reasonable approximation, but the manufacturer's theoretical value is better if you have it.

With the sample data, total OEE comes out at 70.8%: availability of 92%, quality of 94% and performance of 81.6%. The bottleneck is the machine with the highest average cycle time, not necessarily the one that stops most.
